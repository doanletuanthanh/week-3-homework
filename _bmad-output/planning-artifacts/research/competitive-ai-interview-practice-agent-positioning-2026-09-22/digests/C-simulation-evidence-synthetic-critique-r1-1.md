## D4: Does hidden-information role-play work for skill training?

### Simulated/standardized patients (SP) and simulation overall

- **SP-based teaching beats traditional formats on communication skills, with a medium pooled effect: SMD 0.74 (95% CI 0.37 to 1.11), n = 2061, 10 studies meta-analysed out of 21 (about 2500 participants). Heterogeneity was high.** | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39692563/ | Simulation in Healthcare ("Employing Simulated Participants to Develop Communication Skills in Medical Education: A Systematic Review") | 2024 (online Dec 2024; searches run to Feb 2023) | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium (numbers from abstract snippet; full text not fetched) | effect-size. Note: this is about SPs in general, not the hidden-information mechanism specifically.
- **Kaplonyi et al. 2017: many studies reported statistically significant third-party-rated gains in communication after SP-based education, but outcomes were too varied to pool in a meta-analysis.** | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28833360/ (also https://asmepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/medu.13387) | Medical Education | 2017 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium | effect-size (narrative only).
- **Compared with no intervention, technology-enhanced simulation had large effects on knowledge, skills and behaviour, and moderate effects on patient outcomes** (Cook et al., JAMA 306:978-988; 609 studies). | https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/1104300 ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK81848/ | JAMA | 2011 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium. The PDF could not be parsed this run. The exact pooled effect sizes I believe it reports (about 1.20 knowledge, 1.09 skills, 0.79 behaviour, 0.50 patient) are **unverified this run**. | effect-size.
- **Instructional design features matter within simulation. For skills outcomes, feedback gave a pooled ES of 0.44 (80 studies, p<0.001) and repetitive practice 0.68 (7 studies, p=0.06, not significant). Features in order of effect: range of difficulty, repetitive practice, distributed practice, cognitive interactivity, multiple strategies, individualised learning, mastery learning, feedback, longer time, clinical variation.** | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22938677/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.3109/0142159X.2012.714886 | Medical Teacher (Cook et al.) | 2013 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium (snippet) | effect-size/mechanism. Relevant to design: **feedback and repetition are the evidenced levers**, not simulation fidelity alone.

### Debriefing and "do-over" (rapid cycle deliberate practice, RCDP)

- **RCDP means "pause, debrief, rewind and try again": the case is split into segments with micro-debriefs, then learners repeat.** The 2017 systematic review found 15 heterogeneous resources, no meta-analysis, "limited and inconsistent" results, and no data on retention or transfer to practice. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540142/ (PMC5441688) | Cureus | 2017 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium | mechanism.
- **2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of RCDP in resuscitation: shorter time to defibrillation and to epinephrine than controls, but the certainty of evidence was VERY LOW (risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision).** | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38757054/ (PMC11096743) | Resuscitation Plus | 2024 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium | effect-size. Caveat: this is procedural and team resuscitation, not conversational interviewing. **No RCDP evidence for interview or communication skills was found.** "Replay from the missed moment" is analogous to RCDP, but the analogy is not directly tested.

### LLM-based simulated patients (2024 to 2026)

- **Systematic review of 39 studies on LLM virtual patients for history-taking: moderate risk of bias, inconsistent metrics, small cohorts, poor dataset transparency. Hallucination is a named challenge. There is no multimorbidity representation.** | https://medinform.jmir.org/2026/1/e79039 (PMC12811743) | JMIR Medical Informatics (Li and Lutfi) | 2026-01-02 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium-High | mechanism/critique.
- **RCT (n = 84, single centre, ophthalmology): trainees using an LLM digital patient gained 10.50 points more on history-taking assessment than those taught by traditional methods, and also showed improved empathy.** | https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01841-6 | npj Digital Medicine | 2025 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium (snippet) | effect-size.
- **Double-blind RCT (n = 21; 10 vs 11), Brügge et al.: ChatGPT-3.5 patients running on scripted case prompts, all students practising, with the intervention adding structured AI feedback. Feedback improved clinical decision-making: F(1,20)=4.44, p=.049, partial eta squared 0.198. The "focusing questions" subdomain was not significant. The authors warn that LLM inaccuracies can create misconceptions and recommend feedback on process, not medical content.** | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605890/ | BMC Medical Education | 2024 | accessed 2026-09-22 | High (fetched), but the sample is tiny | effect-size/mechanism.
- **RCT (n = 94 novice counsellors), Louie, Shah, ... Brunskill, Yang: LLM-simulated practice plus LLM feedback improved client-centred microskills (reflections, questions). The practice-only group stagnated, and its empathy declined over time, significantly below the feedback group.** | https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02428 | arXiv (Stanford; v2 revised 2026-03-09) | 2025/2026 | accessed 2026-09-22 | High (fetched; preprint) | effect-size/mechanism. **Most decision-relevant study: practice on an AI persona without good feedback can entrench bad habits.**
- **Robustness benchmark of a gpt-4o-mini virtual patient (5 synthetic cases, 10-turn fixed protocols). In the clean condition, case fidelity F1 was 0.947, yet forbidden-information leakage (premature disclosure of diagnoses or test results) still occurred at about 0.60 events per episode. A direct prompt-injection attack collapsed fidelity to 0.098; a defense restored it to 0.945.** | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13278464/ | Cureus (Harada) | 2026-05 | accessed 2026-09-22 | High (fetched; single-author benchmark, not learners) | mechanism. **This is direct evidence that "fixed hidden information released only when asked" is not reliably enforced by prompting alone.**

### Analogues: requirements elicitation, counselling

- **Novice requirements-elicitation interviews (role-played customers; 110 students/28 groups plus 138 students/34 groups): 34 mistake types in 7 themes (question formulation, question omission, interview order, communication, analyst behaviour, customer interaction, teamwork/planning). Students struggled most with formulation, omission and order, and did not improve across three interviews without the reflective intervention.** | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00766-019-00313-0 | Requirements Engineering (Springer; Bano, Ferrari, Zowghi et al.) | 2019 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium (snippet) | mechanism. This is a useful rubric seed: "question omission" maps directly to "missed moment".
- **A RAG-grounded automated stakeholder (n = 69 students) compared with a human faculty role-player: the RAG stakeholder yielded higher-quality elicitation questions and more complete feature identification, but students rated the human stakeholder as more realistic and engaging. Grounding in instructor case materials was used specifically to curb hallucination and inconsistency.** | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12576 ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00766-026-00470-z | Requirements Engineering 31:14 (Binkhonain and Alharbi) | 2026 | accessed 2026-09-22 | High (abstract fetched) | effect-size/mechanism. **The closest analogue to a fixed-hidden-info persona.**
- LLMREI (33 simulated stakeholder interviews) automates the interviewer side, not training. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02564 | arXiv | 2025 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Low relevance | mechanism.

## D5: Synthetic users as replacements for research, and priming risk

- **NN/g (Rosala and Moran) compared the Synthetic Users platform and ChatGPT against three of their own real interview studies. Failure modes found: sycophancy and idealisation (synthetic users "completed all courses"; real users completed 3 of 7 and dropped out), long unprioritised feature lists, misreported behaviour (praised forums that real users found "contrived"), and uncritical concept praise (a drone concept called "a game-changer"). NN/g says the tools are acceptable for hypothesis generation, desk research and *piloting interview guides*.** | https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/ | Nielsen Norman Group | 2024-06-21 | accessed 2026-09-22 | High (fetched; practitioner study, small, qualitative) | critique. The "piloting interview guides" carve-out is compatible with a practice-only positioning.
- **Wang et al.: across 4 LLMs and 3,200 human participants (16 identities), LLMs "acting as" a demographic misportray it (they echo out-group views of the group) and flatten within-group diversity.** | https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-00986-z (arXiv 2402.01908) | Nature Machine Intelligence | 2025 | accessed 2026-09-22 | High (abstract-level) | critique/bias-evidence.
- **Using LLMs as synthetic replacements for survey respondents is perilous (Bisbee et al.).** | https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/synthetic-replacements-for-human-survey-data-the-perils-of-large-language-models/B92267DC26195C7F36E63EA04A47D2FE | Political Analysis | 2024 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium (title/abstract only; I did not verify specific numbers this run) | critique.
- **Persona homogeneity: LLM persona intra-group cosine similarity of 0.94 to 0.99 vs 0.56 to 0.68 for humans. Grounded personas covered only 4.6 to 9.4% of the real-user neighbourhood.** | https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/the-persona-fidelity-gap-behaviorally-grounded-llm-personas-still-compress-real/wrk_acd235a93401608ce56a2a2fd5a50754 | Lacuna (unclear venue/peer review) | 2026? | accessed 2026-09-22 | Low (downgraded: unknown publisher, numbers from snippet) | bias-evidence. Related: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10659v1 ("When Can Digital Personas Reliably Approximate Human Survey Findings?"; population-level alignment possible, individual-level and multivariate structure fail), Medium-Low, snippet only.
- **Interactive virtual personas in design were "useful, but a bit too optimistic". Designers raised concerns about bias, over-optimism and loss of authenticity without real stakeholders.** | https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581925002034 | International Journal of Human-Computer Studies | 2025 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium-Low (title/snippet) | critique.
- **Confirmation bias in interviewing (classic): Snyder and Swann (1978). Interviewers given a hypothesis (introvert vs extravert) chose confirming questions, and interviewees then behaved in hypothesis-confirming ways.** | http://www.communicationcache.com/uploads/1/0/8/8/10887248/hypothesis-testing_processes_in_social_interaction.pdf | JPSP (reprint) | 1978 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Medium (snippet; classic finding) | bias-evidence. Contested: Pennington 1987 reported an "empirical refutation" in face-to-face interaction (https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.2044-8309.1987.tb00784.x). Sackett 1982 on employment interviewers' impressions and questioning strategy (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1744-6570.1982.tb02222.x). Hill et al. 2008 on confirmation bias in suspect interviews (https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1348/135532507X238682). All Medium-Low (abstracts only).
- **Designer confirmation bias toward familiar personas: in a multi-persona AI evaluation tool, experienced cyclists engaged more with persona feedback that matched their own perspective.** | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15671 | arXiv (StreetDesignAI) | 2026 | accessed 2026-09-22 | Low (snippet) | bias-evidence (indirect).

**Inference, not evidence:** If a practice persona embodies a particular user archetype, the confirmation-bias literature suggests learners could carry expectations of that archetype into real interviews and ask confirming questions. Combined with evidence that LLM personas are sycophantic and flattened, this is a plausible risk. **It has not been demonstrated empirically for AI-persona practice.**

## Looked for but could not find

- **Direct evidence that practising on AI personas primes or anchors learners' assumptions about real users. None found.** The claim rests on analogy to the interviewer confirmation-bias literature, which is itself contested (Pennington 1987).
- **Isolated evidence for the hidden-information mechanism, i.e. case info released only when asked vs freely available. Not found.** SP effect sizes cover SP role-play as a whole. The Harada 2026 benchmark shows the mechanism is hard to enforce with LLMs (about 0.6 leaks per episode even in the clean condition), but it does not measure learning impact.
- **Evidence for "replay from the missed moment" in conversational skills.** RCDP evidence exists only for resuscitation and procedural skills, at very low certainty.
- **Evidence on transcript-turn-cited feedback specifically, vs global feedback.** Not found. The feedback evidence (Cook 2013 ES 0.44; Louie 2025/26; Brügge 2024) supports feedback in general, not citation granularity.
- Transfer of LLM-sim gains to real patients or real users: not demonstrated in any study retrieved.
