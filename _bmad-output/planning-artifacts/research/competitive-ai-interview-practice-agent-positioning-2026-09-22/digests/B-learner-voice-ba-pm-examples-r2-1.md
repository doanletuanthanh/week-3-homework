## Pattern catalog additions (Q4: approval-chain, threshold and exception speech)

Source for all rows: Boulding H., Hinrichs-Krapels S. (2021). "Factors influencing procurement behaviour and decision-making: an exploratory qualitative study in a UK healthcare provider." *BMC Health Services Research*. 15 requisitioners at a London NHS Trust. Full text via Europe PMC: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8512597/fullTextXML (PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34645449/). Accessed 2026-09-22. Confidence H (peer-reviewed, verbatim participant quotes). The authors name these workarounds: stockpiling, relying on relationships, bypassing procedures to save time, buying outside agreements, and re-delegating purchasing.

| Pattern | Example (verbatim, participant code) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Single approver bottleneck (hidden dependency on one person) | "There's one member of staff who approves my orders. When she's not in then whatever I've ordered just waits until she's around to approve it" (P4) | Boulding & Hinrichs-Krapels 2021 |
| Newly added approval step slows the flow | "Now my orders have to be approved which has gummed the works up. Some people are very quick, but I've had orders that have been on more than a week" (P10) | same |
| Approver unavailable, so the request stalls | "What if that person is in a meeting and you cannot get in touch with him, then you are at a loss, delayed" (P12) | same |
| Rule-defying (the as-is process departs from the documented one) | "I am defying all the rules, when it comes to ordering I do my ordering because I know it's right" (P10) | same |
| Emergency self-authorisation, going around the system | "The stuff didn't arrive on the Monday and I had to make my own decision…. I phoned the company, submitted my name to them" (P12) | same |
| Relationship channel replaces the formal channel | "I have a very good relationship, so I can phone up any of these suppliers and talk to their managers" (P1) | same |
| Informal re-delegation of purchasing | "I assist the matron…because I know at the moment we're quite short staffed, and if they want something done quickly" (P3) | same |
| Process failure leads to a costly substitute (downstream exception) | "If we have no [bus] tickets we have been known to have to call an ambulance for someone" (P7) | same |
| Request dies in the queue | "[A request for children's toys] took about 3 months. I had to email, and then it got cancelled" (P7) | same |
| Clinical priority overrides cost rules | "They're very patient oriented and they don't normally care too much about the money bit" (P14) | same |

Not found: an explicit monetary-threshold quote (e.g., "anything under X I just sign off"). This study gives approver-bottleneck and bypass speech, not threshold speech.

## Interpretation (tentative, evidence-bounded)

- **The pain is real for at least one learner, but it looks like anxiety plus hindsight mistakes, not an explicit demand for feedback.** The only primary learner account (#1 to #5) shows nervousness, fumbling, and confirmation bias that she found only later. She improved "with practice". Feedback appears only as a late, painful discovery (#4). That fits a need for earlier, lower-stakes feedback, but that is my inference, not a stated wish.
- **Prevalence is not established.** Round 1 and round 2 together give about 6 UX/PM learner voices, mostly from one community type (Medium Bootcamp and Substack). HN adds practitioner observations. That does not meet the bar of two independent communities of learners stating the pain.
- **BA and VN learner voice is still missing.** VN and BA sources place the difficulty on the stakeholder ("customers can't express what they want") rather than on the interviewer's skill. That framing matters for positioning. The one VN acknowledgement of junior skill gaps (#11) comes from a practitioner, not a learner.
- **Q4 is well served.** One peer-reviewed study gives 10 verbatim quotes that cover approver bottlenecks, bypasses, and gaps between documented and actual process. These are usable as realistic hidden-information patterns for personas.
